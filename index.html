Recruitment screening

Check applications for AI writing before you accept them.

Paste an applicant's answers and a few of their Discord messages. The detector marks the patterns AI tends to leave, compares the form with how the applicant normally writes, and gives you follow-up questions to settle it.

  • Private. Everything runs in your browser. Nothing you paste is uploaded or stored on a server.
  • Shows its evidence. Every flag is marked in the text, so you can explain a decision.
  • Built for follow-up. A high result means "verify in person", never "deny".

    How it works

    Nine signals, each with its reasons shown

    No single signal means much. The result rises only when several independent signals agree. The index counts patterns; it is not a probability.

    Different from their Discord

    High weight

    Compares vocabulary level, AI-typical words, dashes, lowercase "i", skipped apostrophes and slang between the form and their messages. A jump in vocabulary is hard to fake.

    AI-typical vocabulary

    High weight

    Words chatbots overuse, such as furthermore, foster, paramount and thrive, counted per 100 words.

    Pasted formatting

    High weight

    Literal **bold** or # heading markers. Google Forms doesn't render them, so they usually come from copying a chatbot reply.

    Stock application phrases

    Medium weight

    Phrases like "valuable asset", "commitment to excellence" and "this experience taught me".

    Even sentence rhythm

    Medium weight

    People mix short and long sentences. AI text tends to keep them all about the same length.

    Little concrete detail

    Medium weight

    Few names, numbers, dates or division terms. Generic answers that could fit any group are typical of AI.

    Em dashes

    Low weight

    A common ChatGPT habit. It counts for more when the applicant never uses dashes in Discord.

    Lists of three

    Low weight

    Repeated "X, Y, and Z" lists of qualities, a pattern AI leans on.

    No casual traces

    Low weight

    Flawless capitalization and punctuation. Weak on its own, since people tidy up for applications.

    Procedure

    Using results fairly across divisions

    Reading the result

    • Low: process the application normally.
    • Read closely: some patterns showed up. Compare with their Discord messages yourself before deciding.
    • Verify in person: several independent patterns showed up. Run the follow-up before you accept or deny.
    • No tool, paid or free, can prove AI use from text alone. Deny on the follow-up, never on the score.
    • Formal writers, non-native English speakers and Grammarly users can trip some signals. The Discord comparison separates them.

    Follow-up steps

    1. Open a ticket with the applicant and tell them it's a standard verification step.
    2. Ask two or three of the generated questions. Watch for hesitation over their own words.
    3. Give them one new question to answer in the ticket within 5 minutes. Paste the reply into step 3 and run it again.
    4. Decide on the full picture and post the copied report with your decision in your staff channel.

    Make the form harder to fake

    • Ask for things only members know: "Name a training you attended, who hosted it, and one thing you learned."
    • Ask for a moment, not an opinion: "Describe the last raid you were in and one mistake your team made."
    • Add a notice: "AI-written answers are denied. You may be asked to explain any answer in voice."
    • Use a scenario that needs your SOP. AI can't guess your internal rules.
    • Calibrate: run 5 to 10 older applications and a few ChatGPT-written ones through it to learn your normal range.
